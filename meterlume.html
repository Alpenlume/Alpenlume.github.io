01Shadows and highlights

Metering the way the zone system does.

Measure the darkest detail and the brightest. Meterlume places them on the zone scale for your film — negatives for the shadows, slides for the highlights — and says, in stops, if the film cannot hold the whole scene.

02After dark

The number a phone gets wrong.

Past a second, film behaves as if it were slower, and the gap grows fast: a metered eight seconds on Tri-X is fifteen in practice. Meterlume adds it, from each film’s published data, and tells you how much it added.

Questions people ask

The iPhone camera meters continuously and reports its own aperture, shutter and ISO — and those three numbers are the exposure equation. Meters from different makers disagree by a fraction of a stop, because they use slightly different calibration constants, so Meterlume lets you set an offset against a meter you already trust, in thirds of a stop.

A reflected meter makes whatever it reads middle grey. So you measure the darkest area that must keep its detail and the brightest, and Meterlume places them on the zone scale for the film you are using: negatives are exposed for the shadows, slides for the highlights. If the scene holds more contrast than the film can, it tells you by how many stops — before the frame, not after the roll is developed.

What this app does not do

It is a reflected-light meter, like the one inside a camera: it reads the light coming off the scene, not the light falling on it. It does not know your lens’s real transmission, and the long-exposure figures come from the film makers’ own published data — a dependable starting point, and still worth a bracket for anything that matters.

Privacy policy

It collects nothing, sends nothing and has no account.

Your film, your settings and your calibration live in the app on your device. There is no server and no account.

What is collected

Nothing. This app has no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising identifier, no crash reporting service and no tracking of any kind. No profile is built and nothing is shared with anyone.

Network use

This app has no server of its own: nothing to sign in to, and nothing uploaded to the developer, ever. What it does with the internet — for most of these apps, nothing at all — is set out under “On your device” above.

Two things do use the internet, and both are somebody else's. The App Store does, when you buy, subscribe or restore. Nothing is sent unless you press send, and this app never learns where it went.
