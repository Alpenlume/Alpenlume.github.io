How to use it.

Six things, in the order you would actually do them on the first evening. None of them needs an account and none of them needs a signal.

  1. 01

    Set what must not be eaten

    On first launch, choose a passcode and then tick the allergies and diets this household lives with. The passcode is asked for only when that list changes, so nobody edits it by accident. Write the recovery code down: there is no server, so nobody can look it up for you.

  2. 02

    Write your list, however you like

    Type “milk, 2 kg potatoes, 500 g beef 5% fat” and it becomes three lines with the right quantities. Or hold the microphone and say it. Or hold a product in the camera’s square: its name appears live, read off the packet or recognised from the picture, and one tap adds it.

  3. 03

    Shop by aisle, not alphabetically

    The list groups itself the way a shop is laid out: produce, bakery, dairy, meat, pantry. Tick things off as they go in the trolley. Anything on your exclusion list is refused as you add it, with the reason.

  4. 04

    Cook something from what you have

    Open “What can I cook” and Fornela ranks its 631 recipes by how few things you are missing, tells you what to buy to finish the dish, and walks you through the cooking. You can type what is in the fridge instead of photographing it.

  5. 05

    Check what a dish comes to

    Any recipe or list shows its energy and macronutrients. If you say a fat percentage — “beef 5%” — that figure is used instead of the shelf average, because 5% and 20% mince differ by nearly double.

  6. 06

    Send it to whoever is cooking

    Add your own photograph of the finished dish, then one button sends the picture and the whole recipe to family and friends through whatever you already use — Messages, WhatsApp, mail. Any recipe also prints as a one-page PDF with the allergy information first.

Questions people ask.

Can I rely on it for a serious allergy?+

No, and the app says so on the screen where it matters. Fornela matches names against a list you wrote yourself. It cannot know what is in an unlabelled dish, it cannot detect contamination in a kitchen, and it is not a substitute for reading a label or asking.

Treat it as a memory aid and nothing more. Read the packet. Every time.

Does it tell me how much I should eat?+

No, and it never will. Fornela shows what is in a food — energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat — the way a packet does. It has no opinion about what you should eat, does not ask your age, weight, height or sex, sets no daily target, and does not add anything up across a day.

It is not a medical device and gives no dietary advice. For that, see a doctor or a dietitian.

Where do the nutrition figures come from?+

USDA FoodData Central — the United States Department of Agriculture's public food composition database. Figures are per 100 g of the generic, raw food.

They are approximate and the app says so wherever it shows one. Real food varies by variety, season and preparation, and a branded product can differ by a third. Only the label on the packet describes what you actually bought.

What happens if I forget my passcode?+

Use the recovery code shown when you first set it up. This app has no server and no account, so nobody — including us — can look it up for you. You can see the code again later in the exclusion list settings, after entering your passcode.
